# Safety

Non-negotiable safety practices for the rocket program. Read before doing anything with propellant.

## Personal

- Safety glasses (Z87+ rated) at all times when handling energetic materials
- Face shield for motor static tests
- Hearing protection for static tests (motors are loud)
- No open flames, sparks, or hot work near propellant
- Cotton or natural-fiber clothing (no synthetics that melt)

## Workspace

- Mix and cast propellant in a clean, dedicated space
- No smoking, no flammable solvents
- Adequate ventilation — some propellant components are toxic (especially perchlorates)
- Fire extinguisher (Class D for metal fires if working with aluminum; ABC for general)
- First aid kit visible

## Propellant storage

- Store oxidizers and fuels **separately** until ready to mix
- KNO3: dry, sealed container, away from combustibles
- Sugar: dry, sealed
- Mixed propellant: use promptly; do not store in large quantities
- Ammonium perchlorate: highly regulated in some jurisdictions, check law

## Static test range

- Clear line of sight to motor
- Minimum 20 m standoff (more for larger motors)
- No people or property in line of fire or in likely shrapnel zone
- Sand/dirt backstop behind motor
- Fire suppression available
- Communication with observers

## Failure modes

- **Casing rupture** — overpressure, material defect. Result: shrapnel. Mitigation: pressure relief, case inspection, standoff distance.
- **Nozzle ejecta** — nozzle failure, throat erosion. Result: high-velocity nozzle fragment. Mitigation: mechanical nozzle retention, standoff.
- **Unintended ignition** — static discharge, friction, contamination. Result: burns, fire. Mitigation: bonding, remote handling.
- **Motor instability** — burn rate oscillations. Result: pressure spikes, possible rupture. Mitigation: grain design (avoid large unburned surface area), adequate port-to-throat ratio.

## Legal

- Join Tripoli Rocketry Association (US) or UKRA (UK) for insurance and sanctioned launches
- Verify local laws on motor classes, propellant quantities, launch sites
- FAA Part 101 waivers required for flights above 150 m AGL in the US (or in restricted airspace)
- Some propellants (especially perchlorates) have storage/transport regulations

## Emergency response

- Burns: cool with water 10-20 minutes, do not apply ointments, seek medical attention
- Eye exposure: flush 15+ minutes, seek medical attention
- Fire: evacuate area, call emergency services, use appropriate extinguisher
- Injury: call emergency services, do not move severely injured persons unless further danger exists

## Documentation

Log every significant event (mixing, casting, firing) with:

- Date, time, location
- Materials and quantities
- Procedure followed
- Result (success, failure, anomaly)
- Photographs

This is for your safety and for learning from incidents.
